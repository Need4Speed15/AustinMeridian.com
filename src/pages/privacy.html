1. About FitQuest and this policy

FitQuest is operated by an individual under the Austin Meridian brand, based in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, United States. It is an exercise-based fantasy adventure in development and testing. We use information to provide accounts, save progress, operate social features, deliver optional notifications, troubleshoot issues you choose to share, and support the current advertising test integration.

The intended initial rollout is the United States for people aged 16 and older, with no upper age limit. This is the intended release audience, not a country geofence. The updated signup asks users to declare they are at least 16. Its age choices are “18 or older” or “Under 18,” with an additional 16-or-older confirmation for the latter. This is self-declaration, not date-of-birth or identity-document verification, and it does not create a universal age restriction at the authentication/API level. Older builds may not have these controls.

2. Information the app handles

Accounts and profiles

Supabase provides account sign-in and account recovery. Account information includes your email, account identifier, and authentication/session information. Your adventurer name is separate from your sign-in email. Current hosted settings allow email signup without email confirmation; the manual deletion process therefore verifies access to the registered inbox separately. Sign-in state may be stored on the device according to your Remember me choice. The updated source stores a private adult or 16–17 age band and its update time, plus a device-cached choice for offline use; it does not collect a birth date for this gate. The choice is editable in Account → Privacy.

Workout information and game progress

You enter exercises, repetitions, custom exercise names, and workout preferences. Device saves also hold your character and appearance, equipment, inventory, gold, XP, health, quest and raid progress, streaks, achievements, rewards, and settings. Progress History records workout and game activity supported by your installed build; it does not reconstruct activity from before tracking was introduced.

Signed-in accounts sync a private cloud save through Supabase when connected. Local changes remain on the device until uploaded. Conflicting device saves require a choice; they are not automatically merged. Database access rules restrict a private cloud save to its authenticated owner. This does not mean no service provider processes the information.

Notifications

Local reminders use your device’s notification system. Optional social alerts use a device push token, installation/binding identifiers, timezone, and notification preferences stored in Supabase. Alert delivery uses Expo and the relevant Google or Apple notification service. System settings and Quiet Hours can affect delivery.

Optional reports and diagnostics

Report a Problem creates a draft for you to review, copy, or share. It can include app/build and device configuration, the current screen, equipped gear and encounter state, and up to 12 recent recorded actions if Playtest Recording was enabled. Notes contain what you type. Reports do not automatically upload, attach a screenshot, or include a full save or authentication credentials. The open draft is cleared when closed or when accounts change.

Optional App Diagnostics keeps up to 64 timing/error-category records in session memory while enabled. Turning it off clears those records; sharing a report is your choice. Separate opt-in Playtest Recording can keep up to 2,000 events locally for the current account, outside cloud saves and backups. These events include play dates/times and progression, equipment, purchase, and battle information. You can clear or export that report. Neither feature is an automatic reporting service.

3. What other players can see

Friends and friend-request participants can see a limited social profile: adventurer name, equipped appearance, level, streak, daily completion count, last-reported activity, and shared recent journey accomplishments. Your email, full cloud save, and individual exercise names/repetitions are not included in those views. The inspected implementation has no free-text friend chat.

Current guild members can see limited profiles, ranks, aggregate synced workout/Guild XP contributions, guild notices, the guild icon, and management activity. These guild views remain shared among current members even if they have blocked each other as friends.

Raid party members share guardian health, member names, damage, attack-workout totals, and recent attack receipts. Access to private raid updates ends when membership ends. These views do not expose individual exercise names/repetitions or your full save. On confirmed deletion, the updated implementation removes personal memberships and receipts while preserving de-identified guild workout/XP/day counts and other members’ progress. Guild and raid leadership transfers to surviving members. Prior raid damage remains reflected in guardian health; an empty raid party can remain as unowned history. Known attributable guild activity and name/email mentions in affected groups are removed or redacted. Arbitrary text, screenshots, and copies held by other people cannot be guaranteed discoverable or erasable.

4. Service providers and test advertising

The current app uses Supabase for accounts, cloud saves, and online features; Expo and Google/Apple notification services for optional push delivery; and Google AdMob and the User Messaging Platform (UMP) for Android advertising tests and privacy choices. Development previews may use Expo services to load the app. Support emails are processed through Gmail when you send them.

The Android integration requests Google demo interstitial ads only, including in release-configured test builds. Live monetization is disabled. iPhone, web, and Expo Go do not request ads through this integration. In the updated source, unknown-age and under-18 users cannot initialize, load, or show ads, while advertising privacy options remain accessible. Adult ads remain Google demos, and an active FitQuest+ membership suppresses ads. Before ad SDK initialization, UMP checks whether consent choices are required; the driver requests non-personalized demo ads. Android demo-ad loading, display, closing, and same-screen/audio resumption passed owner phone checks. These are demo-ad results, not approval to enable live advertising. Demo or non-personalized ads do not imply that Google processes no data.

Google’s advertising and consent SDKs may process IP addresses, device/account or advertising identifiers, ad interactions, diagnostic information, and privacy choices. FitQuest does not attach workout details, adventurer names, emails, or cloud-save contents as targeting data in this integration. No Firebase Analytics linking or real billing was added by the lifecycle update. Google’s SDKs may still carry out their own measurement. Device-scoped Google advertising and consent identifiers are not Supabase account records and may remain under device/provider controls after account deletion.

Review Google’s Privacy Policy, Google’s Mobile Ads data disclosures, Supabase’s Privacy Policy, and Expo’s Privacy Policy. The operator verified the Supabase project in us-east-1, East US (North Virginia), on the Free Plan. Provider processing and transfers depend on each service’s actual configuration and policies. The manual business-email trial does not establish universal delivery of authentication or recovery emails. Current log-access and backup limitations are described below.

5. Permissions and your choices

  • Adjust enabled exercises, targets, profile appearance, and other settings in the app.
  • Control reminders and social alerts in Account → Notifications, and revoke phone notification permission in system settings.
  • Use Account → Privacy → Advertising Privacy to request Google’s privacy options when available and required. Those options can require a connection.
  • Review any issue report, note, or diagnostic export before sharing it. Avoid including private information.
  • Keep backup codes private. They contain an unencrypted save. Restoring a backup replaces progress and keeps one local pre-restore copy.
  • Change your self-declared age group in Account → Privacy in builds containing the updated controls.
  • Signing out ends remembered/offline account access; it does not delete game progress or your online account. Use the account-deletion instructions or email route to request removal.

The inspected gameplay does not read Apple Health or Health Connect activity and does not require camera, microphone, precise location, or contacts access. Permissions shown by Expo Go can belong to the host application. This is not a promise about future versions.

6. Retention, security, and deletion

Account and progress records remain while the account exists. In the updated implementation, confirmed deletion removes live authentication identity and related sessions, both main and test cloud saves, private profiles, age band, personal social links/memberships/receipts, device registrations, and queued alerts. The current device’s account-specific saves, restore/conflict/journal copies, playtest records, remembered credentials, and local age choice clear after server confirmation. Other accounts’ saves are preserved. See deletion instructions and retained-data limits.

Implemented retention controls

  • Completion status: an unlinked random-secret digest and completion time support recovery from an interrupted deletion response. Status is available for 30 days. This receipt contains no account ID, email, or save.
  • Deletion integrity: a separate restricted SHA-256 account-ID digest and completion timestamp help prevent erased identities from regaining access after a restore. This is pseudonymous security information, not a full save or email. The maintenance rule removes records older than 12 months; these records begin with the new migration and are not retroactive.
  • Push queues: source rules give queued alerts 24-hour validity, with worker cleanup after expiry plus two days. Stale device registrations have a 30-day cleanup rule. Actual cleanup depends on the worker running; deletion removes linked registrations and queued alerts directly.
  • Invitations: guild and raid invitations have seven-day validity. Expired validity does not itself guarantee physical row removal.

The hosted daily retention job was verified active and completed a successful scheduled run. The 30-day receipt and 12-month integrity-record expiry rules also passed isolated tests. New live records have not yet reached those full ages, so their complete elapsed retention lifetime has not been demonstrated. Logical expiry and successful maintenance do not guarantee physical erasure of every provider-held copy on an exact date.

Owner-managed operating targets

We aim to complete verified email deletion requests within 30 days of receipt, resolving ownership verification promptly and observing applicable requirements. Ordinary resolved support email has an owner-managed removal target of 12 months after resolution. Minimal manual request records have a 12-month cleanup target after resolution, including completion of a deletion request and should contain only restricted receipt, verification, outcome, and completion information. These are operating targets, not measured service guarantees or automatic mailbox cleanup. An owner-operated request, registered-address challenge, verified removal, and completion-email trial passed. The initial request reached the business Spam folder; mail delivery is not guaranteed, and the operator must monitor Spam as well as Inbox.

Copies and provider limits

De-identified shared progress can remain as described above. Offline devices cannot be remotely wiped. Updated signed-in clients check account status on foreground and every 60 seconds, but cleanup depends on server-confirmed removal; an expired token or network failure alone is not proof. Old builds do not have the new cleanup controls. Exported save codes/files, screenshots, unlinked legacy copies, operating-system or provider backups, provider logs, and notifications already handed off for delivery are outside immediate app control.

The current Free Plan lists one day of API/database logs and one hour of Auth audit-log access. These dashboard/plan access windows are not universal physical-erasure deadlines for underlying provider records. See Supabase’s current plan details. No blanket expiry promise is made for internal provider copies.

Current backup and recovery limits

The current project does not include scheduled provider database backups or point-in-time recovery. The operator chose to use no project backups for now; no separate off-site recovery copy or live provider restoration has been demonstrated. Private cloud-save sync and user-exported save codes are not a promise of a managed recovery service. The app does not currently promise recovery from a lost or damaged provider database.

Isolated synthetic restoration tests passed, but no production/provider restore was rehearsed. Before any future database restoration, the operating procedure requires a separate restricted erasure-integrity log and reapplication of relevant deletions before access is reopened. That separate recovery copy is not currently established. Backup availability and recovery practices must be reviewed if the configuration changes. See Supabase’s backup documentation. Account-bound checks and access rules protect private data, but do not eliminate every risk.

7. This website

This static website contains no site-added analytics, advertising, contact forms, embedded social widgets, third-party fonts, or application cookies/local-storage tracking. Selecting an email link opens your email handler; it does not submit a message automatically.

When hosted on GitHub Pages, GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes. This is hosting-provider processing even though the website adds no analytics. See GitHub Pages data collection and the GitHub Privacy Statement. Following an external link is subject to that service’s policies.
